Picture a courtroom. Six lawyers, each defending a different organisation. Every one of them has read your daily notes, line by line.

The person who wrote those notes is not there to explain what they meant. Neither is anyone else who was on shift. All anyone has is the words on the page.

I have stood in that room twice, representing a provider at a coroner's inquest after the death of a person we supported. What I learned there changed how I look at every care record.

Your records will be read by someone who wasn't there

A coroner, CQC, a commissioner, a safeguarding lead, a family's solicitor. Sooner or later, someone reads your notes with no way of asking the writer what they meant.

Most records don't fall short because staff don't care. They fall short because nobody showed the team what a record actually has to prove.

What weak records look like

  • ✓"In room all evening. Looks fine." A year later, nobody can say what staff actually saw.
  • ✓"Settled" and "no change", with nothing anywhere that says what settled looks like for this person.
  • ✓"Afternoon" instead of a time, so the order of events can't be rebuilt when it matters.
  • ✓A concern written down, with no record of what was checked or what the person was told.

More forms won't fix this. Longer notes won't either. The services whose records stand up know what a record has to prove, and their whole team writes to that standard.
